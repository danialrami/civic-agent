# The Problem: Friction in Local Voting

Local elections are notoriously difficult for citizens to track and participate in. Despite being the level of government that most directly affects daily life—school boards, city councils, county commissions, local propositions—voter turnout in local elections consistently lags far behind state and federal elections.

## The Reality for Young Voters

Young voters (ages 18-35) face particular challenges:

- **Notification Gap**: Most local elections don't have robust notification systems. Unlike federal elections which have national media coverage, local elections often pass with little publicity.
- **Timing Confusion**: Primaries, runoffs, special elections, and general elections follow different schedules. In Texas, for example, the primary is in March, runoffs in May, and general election in November—with special elections scattered throughout.
- **Ballot Complexity**: A single ballot might include federal, state, county, and municipal races plus multiple propositions. Understanding what each position does and who is running requires significant research.
- **Rule Variations**: Voting rules vary by state and sometimes by county. Open vs. closed primaries, registration deadlines, early voting periods, and ID requirements differ widely.

## The Cost of Missing Elections

When voters miss local elections:
- Critical local decisions are made by a small fraction of the population
- School board policies, zoning decisions, and tax rates are set without broad input
- Momentum for civic engagement is lost—missing one election makes it easier to miss the next

## The Specific Texas Example

In Texas (where this project originated):
- **March 3, 2026**: Primary elections
- **May 26, 2026**: Primary runoffs (for races without a majority winner)
- **November 2026**: General election

Many voters don't realize:
1. They can vote in either party's primary (open primary system)
2. Runoff elections exist and are critically important
3. Early voting is available (and often underutilized)
4. Local races (DA, county commissioner, school board) are often decided in primaries/runoffs, not the general election

## The Need for a Better System

Citizens need:
1. **Push notifications** for upcoming elections (not pull-based checking)
2. **Personalized ballots** based on their exact address
3. **Candidate research tools** that aggregate platforms and news
4. **Calendar integration** so elections appear alongside other life events
5. **Local nuance awareness** (e.g., "You're in Bexar County, here's your specific ballot")
